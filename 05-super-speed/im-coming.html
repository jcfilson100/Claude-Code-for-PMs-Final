The "I'm coming" button

When a callout offer buzzes, the responder can press Accept or Decline. If they need longer to reach the phone, they can also tap "I'm coming" once. That gives their offer only 30 more seconds. The answer window stays the same for everyone else. Their handler sees "On the way" right away.

We pair the button with a clock fix: the 60 seconds start when the offer reaches the phone, not when it is sent. Clock fix pending Wen

    Why the clock fix row matches the button row: in this week every offer reaches the phone on time, so starting the clock on arrival changes nothing. It only matters when an offer arrives late, like Ironvale's.

    Extra wait is our safety check. It counts the extra seconds a job sits on someone who still won't take it, before it moves to the next responder. We watch it as part of time-to-assign. With 90s for everyone, every offer nobody answers waits 30s longer. With the button, a job only waits longer when someone taps and still can't make it.

    3. What it tells us

    Hypothesis H4: was a miss "too slow" or "not interested"?

    Today a slow responder and an uninterested one look the same in our data. The button splits them on day one. That matters, because our simulator showed the scoring fix only stops the next freeze-out if most misses were "too slow".

    4. The rules

    1. Every offer has Accept and Decline. Too busy? Decline. The job moves on straight away.
    2. "I'm coming" is optional. No tap needed if you can answer in time.
    3. One tap per offer.
    4. Only in the first 20 seconds after the phone buzzes. It works from the lock screen.
    5. It adds 30 seconds.
    6. It changes only that responder's offer. Everyone else keeps the normal window.
    7. If they still miss, it counts as "ran out of time". That costs −0.04 under the proposed scoring, not −0.12 like a "no".
    8. The handler sees "Offer live" for every offer. They see "On the way" only when the responder taps. Not tapping is never reported.
    9. Paired fix: the 60 seconds start when the offer reaches the phone, not when it is sent. pending Wen

    Late delivery is a different problem

    If offers reach some phones late (H2, unconfirmed), the button only helps part way. The responder gets less time than everyone else, and an offer that arrives after 60 seconds has nothing left to tap. The button alone is a bandage. The real fix is to start the clock when the offer reaches the phone. That is why we pair them. Try "Offer arrived late" with the third rule set. Clock fix pending Wen
